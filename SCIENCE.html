INSPIRED SCHOOL OF TECHNOLOGY

SCIENCE WEEKLY ASSESSMENT FOR 8TH CLASS

Think carefully • Apply scientific ideas • Explain your reasoning

Student Information

Assessment Guide

25 questions
15 objective and 10 written-response questions.
Reason it out
Use evidence and explain why, not just what.
How marking works
Objective answers are marked automatically. Written answers are saved in your result summary for teacher review.

Suggested time: 40–50 minutes. Answer every question. For written questions, use complete sentences and scientific vocabulary.

Section A: Celestial Objects

1
Objective · Reasoning

A planet is visible in the night sky, but it does not produce its own light. Why can we still see it?

2
Objective · Critical thinking

Two students say, “The Moon changes shape every week.” Which explanation is most scientifically accurate?

3
Objective · Apply knowledge

A spacecraft travels from Earth towards the Sun. Which statement about the Sun is correct?

4
Objective · Compare evidence

A comet and an asteroid are observed in space. Which feature is most likely to help identify a comet when it approaches the Sun?

5
Objective · Explain a phenomenon

During a lunar eclipse, the Moon becomes darker. What must happen for this to occur?

6
Subjective · Reasoning

A friend claims that stars are small because they look like tiny points of light. Explain why this conclusion is unreliable. Include one reason stars may look small from Earth.

7
Subjective · Compare and justify

Explain the difference between a planet, a star and a natural satellite. Give one example of each.

8
Subjective · Use evidence

A pupil observes that the Sun appears to move across the sky from east to west. Explain what causes this apparent movement and describe how the observation could help someone tell the time approximately.

❤️

Section B: The Heart and Circulatory System

9
Objective · Apply knowledge

A patient has a damaged valve between the left atrium and left ventricle. What is the most likely effect?

10
Objective · Reasoning

Why is the muscular wall of the left ventricle thicker than that of the right ventricle?

11
Objective · Interpret evidence

After running, a learner’s heart rate rises from 72 to 128 beats per minute. What is the best explanation?

12
Objective · Compare structures

Which blood vessel is best adapted for exchanging oxygen, nutrients and waste products with body cells?

13
Objective · Evaluate a claim

A student says, “All arteries carry oxygen-rich blood.” Which example best shows why the statement is incorrect?

14
Subjective · Explain a pathway

Trace the path of oxygen-poor blood from the body to the lungs, then explain how oxygen-rich blood returns to the body. Name the main heart chambers and vessels involved.

15
Subjective · Analyse evidence

Two learners exercise for five minutes. Learner A’s pulse returns to normal after three minutes; Learner B’s takes eight minutes. Give two possible reasons for the difference and explain why this result alone is not enough to diagnose a health problem.

16
Subjective · Design an investigation

Plan a fair investigation to test whether exercise affects heart rate. State what you will change, what you will measure, two factors you will keep the same, and how you will improve reliability.

🧬

Section C: The Human Reproductive System

17
Objective · Understand function

Which statement best describes fertilisation in humans?

18
Objective · Apply knowledge

If an egg is released from an ovary, which structure is the usual site where fertilisation may occur?

19
Objective · Reasoning

Why does the lining of the uterus become thicker during the menstrual cycle?

20
Objective · Sequence events

Which sequence correctly describes early human development after fertilisation?

21
Objective · Evaluate a statement

Which statement about puberty is the most accurate?

22
Subjective · Explain and sequence

Describe what happens from ovulation to implantation if fertilisation occurs. Include the roles of the ovary, oviduct and uterus.

23
Subjective · Correct a misconception

A learner says, “Menstruation means that an egg has been fertilised.” Explain why this is incorrect and what usually happens during menstruation.

24
Subjective · Compare functions

Compare the functions of the testes and ovaries. Explain why both are important for human reproduction.

25
Subjective · Critical thinking

A student is worried because their body is developing earlier or later than some classmates. Give a scientifically accurate and supportive response, including one reason development may differ and one trusted source of help if they have concerns.
